English:
Things are moving along—slowly, but steadily—which Fr. Jack agrees is a good thing. Over the past several weeks, we have been in discussions with a parish in the West Bank, northwest of Jerusalem, called Ein Arik. Ein Arik is a mostly agrarian community known for its free-flowing spring. In the Old Testament era, it was the home of the Archites (Josh. 16:2; 2 Sam. 16:16), and the name itself means “Spring of the Archites.” In addition to the Roman Catholic church, there is also an Orthodox Christian church and a mosque in the center of the village, which is said to have the second-tallest minaret in the Holy Land. The population of Ein Arik is roughly split between Muslims and Christians. The village is known for its olive and fruit trees, as well as a rare public swimming pool. The parish in Ein Arik is currently in significant need of student scholarships for its school, as many residents are out of work. There are still severe travel restrictions due to IDF blockades, making it nearly impossible for people to work outside the village. These blockades have been in place since the Gaza war began.

We are now arranging a call where Fr. Jack will meet Fr. Firas Abedrabbo for the first time to discuss ways our parishes might connect. From the start, there is a shared understanding that this partnership is not just about fundraising, but about strengthening one another in faith and improving each other’s lives as Christian communities. The conversation will focus on practical ways to do that and the order in which we might move forward.

Some initial ideas—based on our experience with a former parish partnership that has continued for many years:
- Including each other in the Prayers of the Faithful
- Sharing notices in parish bulletins about activities in each community
- Creating a display in the parish hall with photos of parish life, students, and the school
- Organizing a pilgrimage visit from Corpus Christi
- Connecting catechism classes or Scout groups in each parish as pen pals or through similar activities
More to come as these conversations continue.
